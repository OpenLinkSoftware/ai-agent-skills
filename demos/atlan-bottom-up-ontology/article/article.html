AI Ontology: Bottom-Up Doesn't Mean Starting From Scratch

Emily Winks mined local meaning from real systems — then noticed the over-correction: inherited representation standards were skipped, and validity intervals never made it onto the claim. This page is the RDF narrative; the demo shows how Virtuoso keeps bottom-up evidence and inherited form together.

Thesis

Right

Bottom-up mining

Discover local meaning from warehouse, CRM, BI, and catalog — not from a hand-authored enterprise model nobody can reconcile with tables.

Over-correction

Skipped inherited form

SKOS, RDF-star/reification, named graphs, OWL/SHACL, Dublin Core dates — standards for how meaning is represented.

Lesson

Two different questions

Where definitions come from ≠ how they should be represented. Answer the first bottom-up; inherit the second.

Discover meaning locally. Do not reinvent how meaning is represented.

Three old arguments (already in the design)

1. Open / closed world

Keep competing claims during discovery; govern which definition an agent may use after human review.

2. Reification

Treat the claim as an entity. RDF 1.2 / RDF-star attaches source, confidence, and a timestamp — the article took the first two.

3. Partitioning

Named graphs give addressable boundaries for authority, access, and provenance — partially resembled, not fully used.

The missing dimension was time

Every claim carried a source and a confidence. None carried a validity interval.

Detecting that a source changed is not the same as describing the period before the change. Without validFrom / validTo, “last quarter” silently assumes today’s definition also held then.

Fix: two dates and a rule for who sets them — inherited form, not a new invention.

Did we build an ontology?

Authority control (partial)

Preferred + alternate labels resemble SKOS — provenance/date of establishment still thin.

Missing axioms

Without OWL constraints and SHACL shapes, the honest claim may be “a careful account of tables, joins, and metrics.”

Inheritance lesson

No inherited ontology will tell you how this deal desk computes win rate. That meaning is local. The error is reinventing labels, reification, partitions, and time instead of reusing SKOS, RDF-star, named graphs, OWL/SHACL, and Dublin Core dates.

How Virtuoso closes the gap

Same truth · three languages · inherited form

  1. Bottom-up evidence in DB.kidehen tables — claims with source, confidence, and valid_from/valid_to (DDL, sample data).
  2. Inherited ontology — OWL/RDFS + SKOS + PROV (atlan-cc.ttl) at http://localhost:8890/schemas/atlan-cc/.
  3. RDF Views bridge SQL → graph http://localhost:8890/atlan-cc# (IRI classes, QuadMap).
  4. Query one truth via SQL, SPASQL, and SPARQL — with spasqlqb permalinks on localhost:8890/spasqlqb.
